# Product Vision Document

## What should Kivi mean in someone's life?

Kivi should mean the end of translating yourself before you speak. Most people who live between Hindi and English aren't bilingual in two separate boxes — they think in a blend, and every English-first tool forces a small tax: pause, pick a language, restart the thought. Kivi's job is to remove that tax entirely, and then go further — to become the one interface that sounds like you, specifically, in each context you use it, without you having to explain yourself twice.

## Who should it belong to?

Not "Indian users" as a demographic checkbox, but anyone whose working voice code-switches — the engineer who thinks in English but curses in Hindi, the founder who's formal in email and blunt in Slack, the student writing a PRD in one tab and texting a friend in the next. It belongs to people who've never had a dictation tool that didn't secretly assume they think in clean, monolingual, corporate English.

## Where does it create value competitors don't?

Every competitor we looked at is fighting the same fight: cloud tools (Wispr Flow) are fast and fluent but rewrite you into something smoother and genericized, and trust erodes once the free trial ends. Local tools (Superwhisper) are trusted but demand upfront configuration and manual cleanup — you do the work of teaching them who you are. Nobody has cracked smart and yours at the same time. Kivi already has the raw ingredients — Memory (corrections persist) and Styles (per-app personas) — to be the tool that doesn't rewrite you, it remembers you. That's not a feature; it's a structurally different relationship with the user's own voice, and it's the one place where the code-switching angle isn't a market wedge but a design proof: a tool that can hold two languages in one register per app is already doing the hard version of "sound like me," which makes the easier version (consistent tone in English alone) trivial by comparison.

## Why would someone keep using it?

Because the cost of leaving increases every day they stay. A Kivi that's been corrected fifty times, that knows this is "Slack-casual-Hinglish" and that is "Cursor-technical-English-only," is not a tool anyone wants to re-train from zero on Wispr Flow or Superwhisper. Retention here isn't a habit loop — it's accumulated understanding that doesn't export.

## What must it refuse to become?

Kivi must refuse to become a general-purpose AI writing assistant that offers to improve, rewrite, or add ideas the user didn't ask for. The moment Kivi starts sounding smarter than the user instead of more like the user, it has become exactly the kind of tool the "rewrites what I said" complaints are about — just with better PR. It must also refuse to make multilingual support a settings toggle ("select language: Hindi/English") — that's the English-first tools' whole failure mode, wearing a costume of inclusivity. Kivi's multilingualism has to be structural, not a checkbox in a dropdown.
